# Facebook & Instagram — Ad Concepts

Written as **briefs for a designer**, not finished creative. Each includes the
angle, the hook, the format, and what the visual has to do.

---

## Targeting

**Geo:** Calgary + 40km. Include Airdrie, Okotoks, Cochrane, Chestermere,
Strathmore.

**Cold audience:**
- Age 35–60
- Detailed targeting: small business owners, business page admins
- Job titles: owner, founder, president, general manager
- Interests: trade-specific (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, landscaping, construction
  management), plus business-software interests (QuickBooks, Jobber,
  ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro) — those signal an actual operating business, not
  an aspirational one

**Warm / retargeting:**
- Site visitors 30 days
- Video viewers 50%+
- Lead form openers who didn't submit
- Instagram/Facebook engagers 90 days

**Lookalike:** 1% off your existing client list. Twenty years of clients is a
real asset here — upload it.

**Budget to start:** $30–50/day split across 3 angles. Kill anything under a 1%
CTR after 1,000 impressions.

## ANGLE 1 — The Stale Site
*Highest expected performer. Lead with this.*

**Hook (on-image text):**
> Your website still says you're closed Sundays.

**Body copy:**
> You changed that in 2022. It's still up there.
>
> Most Calgary businesses don't have a bad website — they have a website nobody's
> touched since it launched. Wrong hours. Old prices. A guy on the team who left
> two years ago.
>
> We build you a new one, live in 24 hours, and then we keep it current. Forever.
> Unlimited changes, no change fees, and the price never goes up.
>
> $497 to start. $147/month. You own everything.

**CTA:** Learn more
**Format:** 4:5 static
**Visual brief:** A real-looking (mocked, not a real business) website screenshot
on a phone, with one obviously wrong detail circled in the hi-vis amber —
"Sundays: Closed." Deliberately unglamorous. It should look like a screenshot
someone actually took, not a designed ad.

---

## ANGLE 2 — The Ghost
*Highest emotional resonance. Everyone in this audience has lived it.*

**Hook:**
> Remember your web guy?
> Neither does he.

**Body copy:**
> You emailed twice. Nothing. Now you don't know who has the login, who owns the
> domain, or where it's even hosted.
>
> Twenty years in Calgary. Same phone number the whole time.
>
> New site live in 24 hours, unlimited changes after, price locked for life. And
> if you ever leave, you take the site and the domain with you — I don't hold
> anything hostage.

**CTA:** Learn more
**Format:** 9:16 Reel, 15 seconds
**Visual brief:** Ryan talking to camera, handheld, in a real Calgary location —
a shop, a truck, a job site. Not an office, not a desk, no ring light. The
production value should be *deliberately* low. The whole message is "I'm a real
guy who answers the phone," and polished video actively undercuts that.

---

## ANGLE 3 — The Clock
*The proof-of-mechanism ad.*

**Hook:**
> Live tomorrow. Not in six weeks.

**Body copy:**
> Most Calgary agencies quote 6 to 12 weeks. We're live by this time tomorrow, or
> the rush fee comes back and you keep the site anyway.
>
> The build isn't the expensive part anymore. What you're paying for is speed.

**CTA:** Learn more
**Format:** 9:16 Reel, 10 seconds
**Visual brief:** Screen recording — the countdown clock from the landing page
running, then a hard cut to a finished site going live. Timestamp overlays.
No music, or a single hit. Let the clock do the talking.

---

## ANGLE 4 — The Teardown (tripwire ad)
*Best cold-traffic ROI. Lowest friction.*

**Hook:**
> $47 and I'll show you exactly what's costing you calls.

**Body copy:**
> I'll record a video walking through your actual website and your Google
> listing, and point out everything that's quietly losing you business. Usually
> it's three or four things and none of them are what people expect.
>
> Video and a one-page fix list within 24 hours. Take it to whoever you want —
> it's yours either way.

**CTA:** Sign up
**Format:** 4:5 static or short Reel
**Visual brief:** Over-the-shoulder shot of a screen recording in progress — a
site with red annotation marks on it. Looks like work, not like an ad.

---

## ANGLE 5 — Local Proof Carousel

**Hook:**
> Six Calgary businesses. All still live. All still maintained.

**Format:** Carousel, 6 cards
**Visual brief:** One live client site per card, shown on a phone frame, with the
business name and "live since 20XX." Final card is the offer.

**Why it works:** "live since 2019" is the single most credible thing on the
page. It proves the thing competitors can't fake — longevity.

---

## ANGLE 6 — Seasonal / Weather
*Calgary-specific. Run in September and again in April.*

**Hook (fall):**
> Snow's coming. Is your website ready for the phone calls?

**Hook (spring):**
> Everyone's booking their yard work right now. Can they find you?

**Format:** 4:5 static
**Visual brief:** Recognizable Calgary weather imagery — first snow on a
residential street, a chinook arch. No stock photos of smiling people in
hardhats. Calgarians can spot generic stock imagery instantly and it kills trust.

---

## ANGLE 7 — The Comparison
*Best retargeting ad. Works on people who've seen the offer and are deciding.*

**Hook:**
> $3,000 and six weeks. Or $497 and tomorrow.

**Format:** 4:5 static
**Visual brief:** The comparison table from the landing page, redrawn clean, with
the Website Calgary column in rust and everything else in grey. Simple, readable
on a phone.

---

## ANGLE 8 — Authority
*Use with caution — see the warning below.*

**Hook:**
> Twenty years. Same city. Same phone number.

**Body copy:**
> The work has put me in rooms with some remarkable people. Same attention goes
> into a Calgary contractor's website as anything else I've done.

**Format:** 4:5 static or 30s Reel
**Visual brief:** Understated. Ryan, plus restrained reference to the endorsement
credits.

> **⚠️ Before running this:** get **written permission** from Norman Seeff, Renée
> Taylor, and Jully Black to use their names or likenesses in *paid advertising*.
> A testimonial given for a website is not automatically licensed for ads, and
> the rules around endorsements by public figures are stricter than for private
> clients. Meta can also reject creative that implies celebrity endorsement
> without documentation.
>
> **Also test carefully.** Entertainment-industry authority may not translate to
> a Calgary HVAC owner and can read as "expensive, not for me." Run it as a
> retargeting ad to warm audiences before spending cold budget on it.

## Testing structure

**Week 1:** Angles 1, 2, 4 — cold, $30/day, one ad set each
**Week 2:** kill the lowest CTR, add Angle 3 or 6
**Week 3:** turn on retargeting with Angles 5 and 7
**Week 4:** scale the winner, test Angle 8 to warm audiences only

**Read:** CTR above 1.5% is working. Cost per lead under $25 is good for this
market. Cost per $47 teardown sale under $60 means you can scale — you're
acquiring a qualified buyer at near break-even and making the money on the core
offer.

**Creative refresh:** every 2–3 weeks. Calgary is a small enough market that
frequency climbs fast and fatigue is real.

## Organic Instagram — supporting content

Run alongside paid. The ads convert; this builds the trust that makes them cheap.

- **Before/after Reels** — a stale site, then the rebuild. 15 seconds, no talking
- **Build-in-24-hours timelapse** — screen recording, sped up
- **"What's wrong with this site"** — mini teardowns of anonymous sites, weekly.
  This is your best organic format: it's genuinely useful, it demonstrates
  expertise, and it directly pre-sells the $47 tripwire
- **Client launch posts** — tag the business. They reshare. Free local reach
- **Straight answers** — one common question per week, filmed in a truck or shop

Post 3–4×/week. Consistency beats production value with this audience.
