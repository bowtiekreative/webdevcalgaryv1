# Landing Page Strategy

## The page's single job

Convert cold or warm traffic into one of three actions, in descending order of value:

1. **Pay the $497 rush fee** (fastest close, starts the clock)
2. **Call or text** (owner-operators prefer this over forms — do not bury it)
3. **Submit the form** (slowest, needs a same-day callback to convert)

Everything on the page serves one of those three. Cut anything that doesn't.

## Section order and why

| # | Section | Job | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero + dispatch clock | State the promise, make it feel real | The running countdown turns "24 hours" from a claim into a moving object |
| 2 | Proof strip | Immediate credibility numbers | 20+ yrs, N businesses, 24 hrs, price locked |
| 3 | The problem | Agitate the real pain | Three cards: stale hours, ghosted web guy, edits cost money |
| 4 | **Video endorsements** | Authority transfer | Seeff / Taylor / Black — see caution below |
| 5 | **Text testimonials** | Peer proof | Business owners, not celebrities. This is the row that converts trades |
| 6 | Portfolio — live links | Prove longevity, not craft | Live URLs only. A screenshot proves you built it once; a live URL proves you've maintained it |
| 7 | Comparison table | Reframe the price | Positions against agency and DIY, not against Verve directly |
| 8 | Rush fee work order | Justify the number honestly | Build shown as included; the only priced line is speed |
| 9 | Plans | Core vs Growth | Growth flagged as most-picked |
| 10 | Guarantee | Remove risk | Include the 2pm cutoff language |
| 11 | Close block | Two paths: pay now, or form + call/text/calendar | |
| 12 | FAQ | Kill remaining objections | Lead with "why a rush fee" |
| 13 | Sticky mobile bar | Call / Start, always visible | Non-negotiable — the avatar is on a phone |

## The celebrity endorsement problem — read this before placing them

Norman Seeff, Renée Taylor, and Jully Black are extraordinary authority assets.
They are also **mismatched to a Calgary HVAC owner buying a $147/mo website.**

A contractor seeing three entertainment-industry figures on a local web design
page has one of two reactions:

- *"This guy is the real deal"* — authority transfer, which is what you want
- *"This is not for me / this is going to be expensive"* — which loses the sale

**How to place them so you get the first reaction:**

- Put the **text testimonials from business owners above or alongside** the
  celebrity videos. Peer proof closes; authority proof reassures. Never let
  authority stand alone.
- Frame them as *who Ryan is*, not *who the service is for*. Use a line like:
  "Twenty years of this work has put me in rooms with people like these. Same
  attention goes into a Calgary contractor's website."
- Keep the celebrity block **visually smaller** than the peer proof block.
- **Get written permission before using any of them in paid ads.** A testimonial
  given for the site is not automatically licensed for advertising, and
  endorsement rules for public figures are stricter than for private clients.

**Where they earn their keep:** the *upsell*. Norman Seeff's endorsement is worth
far more selling a $4,000 brand film than a $147/mo website. Consider gating the
video block behind the Growth plan page or the production upsell page.

## Above-the-fold checklist

- Promise readable in under 3 seconds
- Live countdown visible without scrolling on a 390px viewport
- Phone number tappable in the header
- Primary CTA and a call option side by side
- "No contract · No lock-in · You own it" microcopy immediately under the CTA

## Mobile rules

The avatar is on a phone. Assume it.

- Sticky bottom bar: **Call now** | **Start →**
- Every button full-width
- Comparison table scrolls horizontally rather than shrinking to unreadable
- Video embeds lazy-loaded — never let a testimonial reel block first paint
- Test on a real phone on LTE, not a desktop resize

## What to test first, in order

1. **Headline** — "Live tomorrow" vs "Never pay for a redesign again"
2. **Entry price** — $497 rush vs $297 rush vs $97 tripwire-first
3. **Primary CTA** — pay now vs book a call vs free teardown
4. **Celebrity block placement** — above peer proof vs below vs removed entirely

Change one at a time. With low traffic volume, run each for a minimum of 100
sessions before reading anything into the result.

## Tracking to install before launch

- GA4 with events on: `call_click`, `sms_click`, `form_submit`, `checkout_click`
- Meta pixel with a custom conversion on each of the above
- Call tracking number if running paid traffic — otherwise you cannot attribute
- A simple spreadsheet: date, source, action, closed y/n. Do this manually for
  the first 50 leads. It will teach you more than any dashboard.
