Two categories, used differently. See `references/01-landing-page-strategy.md`

# VIDEO ENDORSEMENTS — authority proof

**Function:** establishes that Ryan is not a commodity operator. Reassures. Does
**not** close a trades buyer on its own.

**Placement:** keep visually smaller than the peer-proof block, and never
standing alone. Best value is on the **upsell** pages — these endorsements are
worth far more selling a $4,000 brand film than a $147/mo website.

**⚠️ Permissions:** get written consent before using any of these in *paid
advertising*. A testimonial given for a website is not automatically licensed for
ads, and endorsement rules for public figures are stricter than for private
clients.

# TEXT TESTIMONIALS — peer proof

**Function:** this is what actually closes a Calgary business owner. Give it the
prominent treatment.

**Selection rule:** lead with the ones that prove *responsiveness, longevity and
follow-through*. Those beat the ones that praise design, because the buyer's real
fear is being abandoned after launch — not getting an ugly site.

# What's missing — get these

The testimonials above are strong on **craft, service and responsiveness**. They
are thin on the two things this specific offer claims:

1. **Longevity.** Nothing says "he's been maintaining my site for six years."
   That's the single most valuable sentence you could add. Ask three long-term
   clients for it explicitly.
2. **Speed.** Nothing references a fast turnaround. The 24-hour promise currently
   has no social proof behind it. After your first five rush builds, ask each one
   for a one-line quote about the timeline.

**When filming the video testimonials, coach toward these two:**

- "I sent him a change on a Tuesday and it was live Wednesday."
- "It's been six years and he still picks up."

A clip that says "beautiful website" does nothing for this offer. A clip that
says "he's still here" does everything.
